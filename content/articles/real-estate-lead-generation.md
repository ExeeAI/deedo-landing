Ask ten agents about **real estate lead generation** and you'll get ten lists of sources — ads, open houses, social media, farming, referrals. What you rarely hear is the uncomfortable truth: generating leads is only about a third of the job. The other two thirds — *capturing* them cleanly and *converting* them fast — are where most agents quietly bleed the leads they worked so hard (and paid so much) to create.

This guide covers all three. First the proven sources for generating buyer and seller leads, then the capture and conversion layers that turn that volume into actual clients. Think of it as a system: **generate → capture → convert.**

## Generating Leads Is Only ⅓ of the System

Here's the pattern that keeps agents on the marketing treadmill: pour money into more lead sources, watch the leads come in, and then lose most of them to a slow reply, a fake phone number, or a follow-up that never happened. You don't have a lead-*generation* problem. You have a leak between "generated" and "closed."

![Real estate leads leaking between generation and conversion in a broken funnel](/img/articles/real-estate-leads-leaking-funnel.webp "More lead sources won't help if leads leak between generation and conversion.")

Fix the leaks and every dollar you already spend on generation suddenly works harder. So while the sources below matter, pay just as much attention to the capture and convert sections that follow.

## Generate: The Proven Lead Sources

There's no single best channel — the right mix depends on your market, budget, and stage. Here are the categories that consistently produce, grouped from highest-converting to supplementary.

### Referrals, sphere, and past clients

This is the highest-ROI source, full stop. Per NAR's [Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers](https://www.nar.realtor/blogs/economists-outlook/the-top-10-highlights-from-nars-2024-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers), around 40% of buyers found their agent through a referral and another fifth used an agent they'd worked with before — and roughly two thirds of sellers came through a referral or a past agent. Systematically nurture your database, ask for referrals, and stay top of mind with past clients. Nothing else converts as well.

### Online: SEO, Google Business Profile, and content

Own your local search. A complete Google Business Profile, neighborhood landing pages, market-update content, and a lead magnet like a home-valuation offer capture buyers and sellers actively researching. This is slow to build and compounding once it does.

### Paid: search and social ads

Google Local Services Ads, Facebook and Instagram lead ads, retargeting, and portal advertising buy you speed and volume. They work — but only if your capture and follow-up (below) are tight, or you're paying for leads you'll never reach.

### Social and video

In NAR's 2025 technology survey, agents named social media the source of their **highest-quality leads more often than any other channel** — ahead of their CRM, the MLS, and brokerage websites. Organic Instagram, YouTube property tours, and short-form video build a pipeline that paid channels can't replicate.

### Seller-lead engines: expired, FSBO, and farming

For listing agents specifically, prospecting expired listings and FSBOs, geographic farming, and "just sold" direct mail are direct routes to seller leads — the highest-value inventory in the business.

### Open houses

An open house is a lead-generation event disguised as a showing. Done right, it's one of the few channels that puts you face-to-face with unrepresented buyers. The key is capturing them properly — which brings us to the part most guides skip. ## Free vs. Paid — and Where New Agents Should Start

You don't need a big budget to generate leads; you need consistency. New agents should start with the free, high-ROI channels — sphere and referrals, a complete Google Business Profile, consistent social and video, and open houses (yours or a colleague's) — and add paid ads only once capture and follow-up are dialed in. Paying for leads you can't respond to quickly is the fastest way to waste money in this business.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Real Estate Lead Generation

### How do real estate agents generate leads?

Through a mix of referrals and sphere, online search and content, paid ads, social and video, prospecting (expired/FSBO/farming), and open houses. Referrals and repeat clients are consistently the highest-converting source.

### What's the best way to generate real estate leads online?

Local SEO and a complete Google Business Profile, lead magnets like home-valuation offers, social and video content, and targeted paid ads — paired with instant follow-up so online leads don't go cold.

### How do I generate seller leads specifically?

Prospect expired listings and FSBOs, farm a geographic area with consistent mail and market updates, nurture past clients for referrals, and use open houses to meet potential move-up sellers.

### How can new agents get leads with little budget?

Lead with free channels: sphere and referrals, Google Business Profile, consistent social/video, and open houses. Add paid lead sources only after your capture and follow-up systems are solid.

### What matters more — more leads or faster follow-up?

Faster, more reliable follow-up. Most agents already generate enough leads; they lose them to slow response and poor capture. Fixing conversion usually beats buying more volume.
