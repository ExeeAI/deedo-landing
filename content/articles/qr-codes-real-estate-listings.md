A QR code is the shortest path between a curious buyer and your listing. Someone standing at your yard sign, holding a flyer, or scrolling a listing email can be inside an instant Q&A experience in one tap — no app, no sign-up wall. Done right, that single scan becomes a verified lead.

This guide covers where to place codes, what makes them convert, and how to capture the buyer on the other end.

## Where to put your QR code

The goal is to meet buyers everywhere they encounter the listing:

- **Yard and for-sale signs** — scanned from the curb, 24/7, by people who are already interested enough to slow down.
- **Flyers, brochures, and business cards** — the printed handout keeps working after the buyer leaves.
- **Open-house windows and showings** — a code in the window captures after-hours drive-bys.
- **Listing emails, texts, and social posts** — a deep link does the same job digitally.

Put the code where the intent already is. A sign-rider that says "Scan to ask the AI anything about this home" converts far better than a bare code.

## What makes a QR code actually convert

Three things separate a code people ignore from one they scan:

1. **A reason to scan.** Tell them what they get: instant answers, 24/7, about this specific home.
2. **No friction on the other side.** If the scan opens an app store or a form wall, you've lost them. It should open straight into content.
3. **Something worth their time.** An [AI concierge that answers from the listing's real documents](/articles/ai-concierge-open-houses/) gives buyers an immediate reason to engage, instead of a static PDF.

## Turning a scan into a verified lead

A scan is only valuable if you can follow up. The trick is **phone-first identity**: when the buyer starts asking questions, they authenticate with a one-time SMS code. That does two things at once — it proves the number is real, and it gives you a verified US mobile number to reach them on.

From there, interest is tagged automatically, and the buyers most likely to make an offer surface at the top of your dashboard. You spend your time on the hot ones instead of chasing tire-kickers.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **A code with no context.** Always pair it with a one-line prompt.
- **Linking to a static page.** A scan should start a conversation, not end at a brochure.
- **No lead capture.** If the scan doesn't capture a contact, you've given away information for nothing.

## The takeaway

QR codes work because they collapse the distance between interest and action. Place them where buyers already are, give them a clear reason to scan, remove every ounce of friction, and make sure the scan captures a verified contact. One code on a sign can quietly do the work of a dozen phone calls.
