## Why Texting Wins — and Why It's a Mess on Your Personal Phone

Text messages get opened and answered fast, which is exactly why speed-to-lead and texting go hand in hand (more on timing in our [lead response time](/articles/real-estate-lead-response-time/) guide). ## Get Consent Right First: TCPA Basics for Agents

Texts are regulated like calls under the federal **TCPA**. Getting this wrong isn't a slap on the wrist — statutory damages run **$500 per text**, up to **$1,500 for willful violations**, and each message counts separately.

### Consent comes first

You need the recipient's prior express consent before texting, and **marketing texts require prior express *written* consent.** A text should never be a surprise to the person receiving it. If you bought a list or someone hasn't opted in, don't text them.

### Honor opt-outs and respect quiet hours

Recipients can revoke consent "in any reasonable manner," and you must honor a **STOP/opt-out promptly** — the FCC's 2024 order requires honoring revocation within 10 business days, but good practice (and most platforms) stop immediately. Keep marketing texts within **quiet hours**: federal telemarketing rules set an 8 a.m.–9 p.m. window in the *recipient's* local time zone, and several state "mini-TCPA" laws are stricter (some 8 a.m.–8 p.m., with daily message caps). A safe national default is **9 a.m.–8 p.m. recipient-local.** The FCC's [guide to stopping unwanted texts](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/stop-unwanted-robocalls-and-texts) is worth knowing, and NAR's [telemarketing and cold-calling resource](https://www.nar.realtor/telemarketing-cold-calling) covers agent-specific DNC obligations.

### Register for business texting

Texting leads at scale from a 10-digit number generally requires **A2P 10DLC registration** (your carrier's business-texting program), your first message should identify you and your brokerage, and every campaign must support STOP/HELP replies.

## Real Estate Text Scripts by Scenario

Keep every text short — one to three sentences — personalized, and ending with a single clear question. Here are original templates to adapt (never send a wall of text; if it needs more, call).

### New lead / speed-to-lead

> "Hi [Name], it's [You] with [Brokerage] — you asked about [address] on [site]. Happy to send details or set up a tour. Are you hoping to see it this week?"

### Open-house follow-up

> "Hi [Name], great to meet you at the open house on [street] today. Want me to send the disclosures, or line up a private second look?"

Keep open-house follow-up tight and fast — the [converting open-house visitors](/articles/convert-open-house-visitors-into-leads/) guide covers the full sequence.

### Under-contract / status update

> "Quick update: the inspection is scheduled for [day]. I'll send the report as soon as it's in. Any questions in the meantime?"

Status texting is where organization matters most — see [transaction coordination](/articles/real-estate-transaction-coordination/) for keeping every party aligned.

### Price drop or new listing

> "Thought of you — [address] just dropped to [price], which fits what you were after. Want to take a look before the weekend?"

### No-response re-engagement

> "Hi [Name], no rush at all — are you still keeping an eye on [neighborhood], or has your timeline shifted? Happy to help whenever you're ready."

### Past-client check-in

> "Hi [Name], hope you're loving the new place! Just checking in — reach out anytime you need anything. Always here to help."

## Texting Best Practices

- **Respond fast.** A text sent within minutes of an inquiry dramatically outperforms one sent hours later.
- **Keep it short.** One to three sentences. Brevity reads as respect for their time.
- **Personalize.** Use their name and reference the specific property or neighborhood.
- **One clear ask.** End with a single question so the next step is obvious.
- **Identify yourself.** Name and brokerage in the first message, every time.
- **Know when *not* to text.** Contracts, sensitive documents, bad news, and promotional blasts belong on a call or a secure channel — not an SMS.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Texting Real Estate Leads

### Is it legal for real estate agents to text leads?

Yes, with consent. Texts are regulated under the TCPA, so you need the recipient's prior express consent — and prior express *written* consent for marketing texts — before you text them.

### What are the quiet hours for texting leads?

Federal telemarketing rules set an 8 a.m.–9 p.m. window in the recipient's local time zone, and some states are stricter. A safe default is 9 a.m. to 8 p.m. recipient-local.

### How fast should I respond to a new lead by text?

As fast as possible — ideally within about five minutes. Text response speed is one of the biggest drivers of whether a new lead ever converts.

### How long should a real estate text be?

One to three sentences. If your message needs more than that, it's a phone call, not a text.

### What should I never text a client?

Contracts, sensitive personal or financial documents, bad news, and promotional blasts. Those belong on a call or a secure, purpose-built channel.

### How do I handle a STOP or opt-out request?

Honor it promptly — immediately in practice — and keep a record. Compliant texting platforms process STOP automatically and send a confirmation.
