Expired listings and for-sale-by-owner homes are the most reliable inventory pipeline a listing agent has — because both groups are made up of motivated sellers who already want to sell and just haven't gotten there. Learning **how to get expired listings** and convert FSBOs is one of the highest-ROI prospecting skills in the business, and most agents do it badly enough that the opportunity stays wide open.

This playbook covers where to find these leads, the compliance rules you cannot ignore, how to handle the objections, and — the part that actually wins the listing — how to earn a burned seller's trust.

## Why Expired and FSBO Sellers Are Your Best Inventory Pipeline

An expired listing is a seller who tried and failed — usually because the home was overpriced or under-marketed, not because it's unsellable. A FSBO is a seller so motivated they took it on themselves. NAR's data shows FSBOs have fallen to about **6% of home sales**, an all-time low, and the vast majority of sellers ultimately use an agent. In other words: these sellers are pre-qualified on motivation, and most of them will list with *someone*. Your job is to be that someone. ## How to Find Expired Listings and FSBO Leads

**Expired listings** come primarily from your MLS — set saved searches for expired, canceled, and withdrawn status over the last few weeks, then verify current ownership through public records (which can also reveal motivation, like a relocation or divorce). Paid platforms like REDX, Vulcan7, and Landvoice add skip-traced phone numbers and often bundle DNC scrubbing.

**FSBOs** surface on the portals, Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, and via those same aggregator tools. One caveat worth knowing: aggregator data is only as accurate as the public source the owner posted, so verify ownership before you reach out.

## Before You Dial: DNC and TCPA Compliance

This is the part that gets agents fined, so lead with it. **A FSBO or expired number is not automatically safe to call.**

Per NAR's [telemarketing and cold-calling guidance](https://www.nar.realtor/telemarketing-cold-calling), you generally **cannot call a property owner listed on the National Do Not Call Registry** to solicit your listing services — even if their number is publicly posted on a FSBO ad. The practical rules:

- **Scrub every number against the [FTC's National Do Not Call Registry](https://www.donotcall.gov/)** — and re-scrub at least every 31 days.
- **Call only between 8 a.m. and 9 p.m.** in the recipient's local time.
- **Honor opt-outs immediately** and keep a written compliance plan.
- **Get proper written consent** before any autodialed or pre-recorded outreach, and note that AI/artificial voices fall under TCPA rules too.

There's a limited past-client exception (roughly 18 months after your last transaction with them), but when in doubt, scrub and confirm. (None of this is legal advice — follow your broker and counsel.)

## Expired Listing Scripts and Objection Handling

The mistake agents make on expired calls is leading with themselves. Lead with the *seller's situation* instead. A strong opener acknowledges the frustration without bashing the previous agent:

> "Hi [Name], I saw your home on [street] came off the market. I'm sure that's frustrating after all the effort. I've actually done some homework on why it may not have sold — would you be open to a quick, no-pressure rundown?"

Come with property-specific research (a fresh CMA, an honest read on what went wrong) and ask for a short strategy review, not a hard listing pitch. Common objections and how to steer them:

- **"We had a bad agent."** Don't pile on — redirect to strategy: "The good news is the two most common reasons are pricing and marketing, and both are fixable."
- **"We're not selling anymore."** Clarify whether that's permanent or just paused after a bad experience.
- **"Why should I trust another agent?"** This is the real objection under all the others — and it's where you *show* rather than tell (more below).

## FSBO Scripts and the Commission Objection

FSBO sellers are testing whether they need you at all, so lead with value, not a pitch — offer a free CMA or honest feedback on their listing. The near-universal objection is commission, and the reframe is **net proceeds, not fee**:

> "Totally fair to want to maximize your money — that's exactly my goal too. The real question isn't the commission percentage; it's what you *net* at the end. Homes sold with an agent have historically sold for meaningfully more, and that gap often covers the fee and then some. Can I show you the numbers for your specific home?"

Plan on persistence — a value-first cadence of five to six touches over six to eight weeks converts far better than one call. Most agents quit too early, which is exactly why the opportunity is there.

## You Won the Appointment. Now Win the Skeptical Seller.

Here's the moment that decides everything. A burned expired seller or a go-it-alone FSBO doesn't want more promises — their last experience *was* a promise that didn't pan out. They want **proof of a genuinely better experience.** This is where you differentiate, and where the pitch becomes evidence.

Show them exactly how you'll market and manage the home — the substance behind [a listing presentation that wins](/articles/real-estate-listing-presentation/). This is where Deedo.ai gives you something concrete to demonstrate:

- **An AI Concierge** that answers buyer questions about their home 24/7, grounded in the property's own documents — the direct antidote to "my last agent just threw it on the MLS and disappeared."
- **Digital open-house check-ins** that capture validated buyer data, so you can show the seller real engagement instead of vague activity reports.
- **Property-scoped multi-party chat** that keeps buyers, brokers, and the seller organized and informed — an experience a FSBO simply can't replicate alone.

"Here's exactly how I'll market *and* manage your home" beats any script, because it answers the trust objection with something the seller can see.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is an expired listing?

A listing whose agreement ended without the home selling — distinct from "withdrawn" (pulled by the seller during the term) or "canceled." Expireds are prime prospects because the seller still wants to sell.

### Is it legal to cold-call expired listings and FSBOs?

Only if you comply with DNC and TCPA rules. You must scrub numbers against the National Do Not Call Registry, avoid registered numbers absent an exception, call within permitted hours, and honor opt-outs.

### Why do most listings expire?

Overpricing is the number-one cause, followed by weak photos and marketing, difficult showing access, and poor agent communication — nearly all fixable with a better strategy.

### How many times should I follow up with a FSBO?

Plan on five or six value-first touches over six to eight weeks. Persistence is the edge, since most agents give up after one or two tries.

### How do I answer the commission objection?

Reframe from fee to net proceeds: agent-assisted sales have historically netted sellers more, and show them the specific numbers for their home rather than arguing percentages.

## Turn Motivated Sellers Into Your Next Listings

Expired and FSBO prospecting rewards agents who show up prepared, stay compliant, lead with the seller's situation, and — above all — prove they'll deliver a better experience than the seller had before. Do that consistently and you'll never be short on inventory.
