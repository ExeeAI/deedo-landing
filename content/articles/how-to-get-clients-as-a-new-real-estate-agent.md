Getting your real estate license is the easy part. Getting your first clients — with no database, no track record, and usually no budget — is where most new agents sink or swim. If you're staring at an empty pipeline wondering where your first deal comes from, this guide is your playbook: the free tactics that actually work, the one channel built for rookies, and how to look more professional than agents who've been doing this for a decade.

## The Brutal First-Year Math

Let's be honest about the odds so you can beat them. Per NAR's [Member Profile](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics), agents with two years or less of experience earned a **median of about $8,100** in 2024 and closed a **median of three transactions**. Most of the field is experienced — only around a quarter of members have five years or less. (You'll also hear that "87% of agents fail in five years" — that number is an untraceable industry myth, so don't let it scare you; the real challenge is simpler.)

The agents who don't make it usually aren't short on talent. They're short on two things: out-*hustling* the field, and out-*professionalizing* it. Do both and the math changes fast. Here's how.

## Start Free: Work the Database You Already Have

Your first clients are hiding in your phone. Build a list of everyone you know — aim for 100+ contacts — and send a genuine announcement that you're now a real estate agent. Don't pitch; just let people know, and add a soft ask: *"If you ever come across anyone with a real estate question, I'd love for you to send them my way."*

Your **sphere of influence** is the highest-converting source in the business, and it costs nothing. Follow up consistently, add value, and stay visible. (For the full range of channels, see the [real estate lead generation](/articles/real-estate-lead-generation/) hub.)

## The Rookie's Secret Weapon: Host Open Houses for Other Agents

Here's the tactic tailor-made for new agents: **you don't have listings, so borrow other agents'.** Busy veterans are often glad to hand off their Sunday open houses — and every person who walks through that door is *your* lead, not theirs. A single open house can put you in front of 20 to 50 potential buyers and sellers.

## Direct Prospecting: Door-Knocking, FSBOs, and Expireds

When you have more time than money, direct prospecting pays. Door-knock a target neighborhood with something useful in hand — a simple market report rather than a sales pitch. Work FSBOs and expired listings with a value-first approach; persistence beats experience here, since most agents quit after one or two contacts (see [how to get expired listings and FSBOs](/articles/how-to-get-expired-listings/)). Floor duty at your brokerage and first-time-homebuyer seminars co-hosted with a lender are other low-cost ways to meet clients.

## Out-Professionalize the Veterans

You can't fake a decade of experience — but you can *look* every bit as buttoned-up, which is often what wins the client. Three moves close the gap:

- **Own your online first impression.** Prospects will Google you before they call. A professional headshot, a complete Google Business Profile, and consistent, helpful social posts make you look established.
- **Follow up faster than everyone.** New leads should hear back within minutes, not days — speed is something a rookie can win on immediately.
- **Run transactions like a pro team.** Deedo's **property-scoped multi-party chat** keeps the buyer, their broker, and you in one organized thread, so your first deal feels as smooth as your hundredth. Modern, organized, and responsive reads as *experienced* to a client.

## What to Realistically Expect

Set your expectations so you don't quit right before it works. Most new agents take a few months to land their first client and often close only a handful of deals in year one — that's normal, not failure. If you can, save several months of expenses before going full-time, keep your prospecting consistent, and treat the slow start as the price of building a pipeline that compounds.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do new agents get their first client with no experience?

Start with your sphere of influence, host open houses for other agents' listings, and prospect FSBOs and expireds. These require effort rather than money and put you in front of real buyers and sellers fast.

### How long does it take a new agent to close their first deal?

Commonly two to six months to a first commission. Consistent prospecting shortens it; sporadic effort lengthens it.

### Can I get clients without buying leads?

Yes. The highest-converting sources — your sphere, referrals, and open houses — are free. Paid leads are best added later, once you have cash flow and a follow-up system.

### How do I get listings when I have no track record?

Prospect expireds and FSBOs, farm a neighborhood with consistent value, and win on preparation and a modern, professional experience rather than years in the business.

### How do I host an open house if I don't have my own listings?

Ask experienced agents in your office if you can host theirs. Many welcome the help, and every visitor becomes your lead — capture them with a digital check-in.

## Out-Hustle and Out-Professionalize

There's no shortcut to your first clients, but there is a formula: work the relationships you already have, borrow open houses to meet new people, prospect with value, and present yourself as more organized and responsive than agents twice your tenure. Do that consistently and the empty pipeline fills faster than the statistics suggest.
